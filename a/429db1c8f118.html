Beluga whales filter feed with baleen plates, not teeth

Beluga whales do not have teeth in their mouths. Instead, they possess a unique filter structure known as a baleen plate, according to Prothom Alo. The baleen plate works like a sieve, allowing the animal to strain seawater and capture tiny prey. By filtering the water, the whales feed on small fish, krill and plankton that pass through the plates. This feeding method distinguishes beluga whales from toothed marine mammals and highlights their adaptation to a plankton-rich environment.
